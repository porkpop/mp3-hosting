Design Rationale: Stable Hero · Fast Routing · Dynamic Proof · Deeper Pages

1 · Stable Hero — Anchor, don't overwhelm

The hero is dark, typographically strong, and intentionally short. The headline ("Clear evidence. Safer communities. Privacy first.") establishes purpose and values in nine words. The proof module — two stat cards — provides immediate credibility without turning the hero into a dashboard. The missing-persons stat leads because it is human, emotionally legible, and requires no explanation. Static headline + CTA means the page feels anchored across every visit; CMS proof cards mean the numbers stay fresh without a redesign.

2 · Fast Routing — Help visitors choose, not read

The routing module (§2) appears early in the page — before any narrative sections — because many visitors arrive with a specific task (e.g., "Is Flock right for my police department?"). Six route cards with a short label, one-line value, and a direct link resolve that question immediately. The cards are intentionally plain: the goal is navigation, not persuasion. Visitors who need depth will find it on the destination pages. This structure also makes the homepage resilient to new audiences — adding or renaming a card is a template edit, not a page redesign.

3 · Dynamic Proof Layer — Editorial, not social

The proof layer (§3) is positioned after routing because it rewards visitors who want evidence before committing to a path. The featured card + two smaller cards layout creates hierarchy — one proof point feels prominent, others feel supporting. All content is CMS-driven so the marketing team can swap cards (new case study, new Safe City Index data, new product launch) without touching the template. The design uses editorial visual language — a quiet category tag, a strong headline, a source line — rather than a social feed or news ticker, which would feel noisy and reduce credibility.

4 · Deeper Pages Carry the Full Narrative

The homepage does not attempt to explain every product, policy, or proof point. §4 (Platform) gives three-word concept headings (Detect / Investigate / Respond) and routes to the platform page for depth. §5 (Audience) gives one-stat proof per audience and routes to dedicated audience pages. §6 (Trust) frames the accountability story in one sentence and routes to the Trust Center. This keeps the homepage scannable for first-time visitors while ensuring that readers who want the full narrative have a clear path to find it. The homepage is the front door, not the whole house.

CMS vs Static Split — Why it matters

The strategic design choice to separate static structure from CMS content reflects the two different tempos of this page. The structural elements — hero headline, routing labels, platform framework, CTAs — change rarely and require brand/strategy sign-off when they do. Keeping these static prevents accidental drift and ensures the page's core message stays stable. The proof layer — stats, outcome stories, customer quotes, event cards, trust updates — needs to stay current to feel credible. A homepage that still shows a 2023 stat in 2026 undermines the very proof it is trying to deliver. CMS-driving these cards means the content team can maintain freshness without engineering involvement. The "last updated" date on hero proof stats adds transparency and prevents readers from assuming a static number is live data — a subtle but important trust signal that aligns with Flock's commitment to responsible technology.
